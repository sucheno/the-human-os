In the systems overview, you learned that the endocrine system uses hormones — chemical messengers released into the bloodstream — to coordinate activity across the body. This section goes deeper. We will look at the individual hormones that most affect your daily health, energy, mood, and sleep.

## Why a dedicated section on hormones?

Hormones touch everything. The energy you feel after a meal, the fatigue you feel when you skip sleep, the anxiety you feel under deadline pressure, the hunger that hits at odd hours — all of these experiences are shaped by hormonal signals.

Understanding a few key hormones gives you a surprisingly useful model of how your body responds to food, stress, darkness, and physical activity. You do not need a biochemistry degree. You need to understand what triggers each hormone, what it does, and what happens when the signal gets stuck on.

## What this section covers

Over the next seven articles, we will examine:

- **Insulin and glucagon** — the blood sugar regulators
- **Cortisol** — the stress response hormone
- **Thyroid hormones** — the metabolic thermostat
- **Sex hormones** — estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone beyond reproduction
- **Leptin and ghrelin** — the hunger and satiety signals
- **Melatonin** — the sleep-onset signal
- **Feedback loops** — how the endocrine system keeps itself in balance

Each article builds on the systems knowledge from Section 1. If you have not read the endocrine system overview, that is a good place to start before continuing here.
