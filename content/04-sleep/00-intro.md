Sleep is not downtime. It is not the absence of wakefulness. It is an active, metabolically distinct state during which the body performs critical maintenance — consolidating memories, clearing metabolic waste from the brain, repairing tissues, regulating hormones, and recalibrating the immune system.

You can survive longer without food than without sleep. After 24 hours of sleep deprivation, cognitive impairment is roughly equivalent to having a blood alcohol concentration of 0.10% — above the legal driving limit in every U.S. state. After several days, hallucinations, paranoia, and psychosis can emerge.

## Why a section on sleep?

Everything you have learned so far — the cardiovascular system, the hormonal axes, the metabolic processes, the immune defense — depends on sleep to function properly. Chronic sleep deprivation disrupts insulin sensitivity, elevates cortisol, reduces leptin, increases ghrelin, suppresses immune function, and impairs memory consolidation. It is a single behavior that affects every system simultaneously.

Over the next four articles, we will explore:
