By now you have a working model of how the body's systems operate and how hormones coordinate them. This section is about what fuels the whole machine: food.

Nutrition is not just about calories. The molecules in food serve three fundamental roles:

- **Fuel:** Carbohydrates and fats are the body's primary energy sources, powering everything from brain function to muscle contraction.
- **Building blocks:** Proteins provide amino acids for building and repairing tissues, enzymes, and hormones. Minerals like calcium form structural components of bone.
- **Information:** Vitamins and minerals act as cofactors and coenzymes in hundreds of biochemical reactions. Without them, metabolic pathways stall — even when fuel is abundant.

## What this section covers

Over the next six articles, we will examine:

- **Macronutrients** — carbohydrates, proteins, and fats in detail
- **Fat-soluble vitamins** — A, D, E, and K
- **Water-soluble vitamins** — the B vitamins and vitamin C
- **Essential minerals** — from calcium to zinc
- **The digestion process** — how food goes from plate to absorbed molecules
- **The gut microbiome** — the trillions of organisms influencing your health from the inside

Each article connects back to the systems and hormones you have already learned about. Nutrition does not exist in a vacuum — it is the input that every system depends on.
