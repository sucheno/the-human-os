You now have working knowledge of how your body's major systems operate, how hormones coordinate them, what nutrients fuel them, and why sleep is essential for all of them. This final section connects these topics into an integrated picture.

The key insight of this course is that the body does not have isolated problems. A sleep deficit does not only cause tiredness — it raises cortisol, disrupts insulin sensitivity, increases appetite hormones, suppresses immune function, and impairs memory. A nutrient deficiency does not just affect one organ — it cascades across systems that depend on that nutrient as a cofactor.

## What this section covers

Over the next four articles, we will explore:

- **Exercise physiology** — what happens inside your body when you move, and why physical activity touches every system
- **Chronic stress** — what long-term cortisol elevation does across your cardiovascular, immune, and metabolic systems
- **How it all connects** — specific cross-system interaction examples that tie the course together
- **Building daily habits** — evidence-based routines that support multiple systems simultaneously

The goal is not to prescribe a regimen. It is to show you how the knowledge from the previous sections translates into practical understanding — so that when you make choices about sleep, food, movement, and stress, you understand what is happening at the systems level.
